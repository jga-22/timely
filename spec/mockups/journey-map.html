Timely: the journey from first visit to first quarter

Six stages, one map. For each stage: what the person wants, where they go and what they do, how they may feel, the main reason they might quit, the device that keeps them going, what it costs them to maintain, and how we would know it works. Proposal only, not built. Sample content for discussion.

Companion to spec/ux-journey-map.md. Labels: evidence = seen in the production build; hypothesis = to test; untagged text is a design proposal. Curves are sketches of how a stage may feel, not measured data.

1

Land and understand

User goal
Know in five seconds what Timely is for and what to do first.
Screen and action
Welcome. One button to start, one line on what the next three minutes do. The intro is there if wanted.
Emotion
curious, then unsure it is for me
Main risk of quitting
Reading five new terms before doing anything; or a "start empty" that leads to a blank product.
Motivation device
The dream link in one sentence: your ambition, the week that serves it, and what you ticked. No reward yet.
Maintenance
None.
Success metric
4 of 5 people name a next action in 5 seconds hypothesis.

Today: Guide me, Start empty, "Watch the short intro"; five terms before the first click. evidence

2

Set up, under 3 minutes

User goal
A first plan that reads as their own life, saved only after they accept it.
Screen and action
Five questions (six at most), then a draft plan: Accept plan or Use a starter. Every line is editable.
Emotion
effort, then it reads like me
Main risk of quitting
The first question; a draft that looks generic; a long build with nothing to see yet.
Motivation device
The draft puts the ambition, in their own words, at the top. Each area shows its habit and the reason it is there ("from your answer: Physical").
Maintenance
Once, about 3 minutes. Later edits happen in place.
Success metric
Accepted plan in under 3 minutes (median); fields edited before Accept; starter use hypothesis.

Today: Guide me is 4 clicks and 3 typed fields; the first numbers disagree (66 h, 3 h). evidence

3

First day

User goal
See what is due today and tick one thing.
Screen and action
Home, Today. The next block, one tick, the ring. Nothing else is needed.
Emotion
waiting, then a small win at the first tick
Main risk of quitting
Nothing planned today, so the first screen is empty; a tick that feels like nothing happened.
Motivation device
One quiet line on the first tick. The ring turns into a green check when today is done. Behind this block: the 1-year target it serves.
Maintenance
Under 2 minutes.
Success metric
Home shows a block for today, or names the next one, for every rules-path run; first tick on day 1 hypothesis.

Today: after Guide me, 4 of 7 days land on "Nothing planned today". evidence

4

First week

User goal
A week that looks like theirs, and a rhythm that survives a missed day.
Screen and action
Home, the week so far: seven cells and one sentence. Week only to add a block; Goals only when they want to.
Emotion
a dip on days 2 to 4, then recovery
Main risk of quitting
The likeliest quit window (estimate). Fiddly grid editing; numbers that look like failure after one miss; empty screens elsewhere.
Motivation device
Seven cells, "3 of 5 planned sessions so far", and "a missed session costs one session, nothing else". No streak counter. A quiet line for the first full week.
Maintenance
About 5 minutes once to add blocks, then daily ticks.
Success metric
Back on day 3 without a prompt; ticks on at least 3 days of week one; one entry added or edited hypothesis.

Today: Goals shows 7 of 8 rows as "Add a goal"; Insights says "Tick activities on Home". evidence

5

First month

User goal
See the shape of the month and adjust one part of the week.
Screen and action
Progress: one headline in words, an eight-week trend, the weekly recap with Done for the week. Then Week for one change.
Emotion
the shape shows over the month
Main risk of quitting
One percentage read as a score; a long page that is reference, not a story; a recap that feels like a report card.
Motivation device
The recap closes with one line and "Plan next week". The trend shows what one headline cannot.
Maintenance
About 5 minutes a week for the review (part of the 10-minute weekly budget).
Success metric
3 of 4 weeks with a recap opened or closed; a week changed after a review; Progress read in under 30 seconds hypothesis.

Today: the Insights page is about 1,960 px tall at 1280 with one figure repeated. evidence

6

First quarter or year

User goal
See the ambition move, and decide whether the 1-year target still fits.
Screen and action
Goals for one life area's progress and stage; the ambition ring on Home; Year for the hours by month.
Emotion
a stage moves, slowly
Main risk of quitting
Progress feels too slow; an average hides the weak area; a holiday breaks the rhythm; the quarter passes with no review.
Motivation device
Calm stages per life area from the 1-year target (Starting to Reached); the ambition ring with its method named.
Maintenance
About 20 minutes each quarter to confirm or change the 1-year targets (estimate).
Success metric
1-year target confirmed each quarter; at least one life area moves a stage; still active at day 90 hypothesis.

Today: no stages and no ambition ring; Year shows a red ceiling line. evidence

The weekly loop

Three moments, repeated every week. Each one has a place in the product today; the proposal changes how they are reached, not what they hold.

Plan, once a week

Look at the week, add or move a block

Week tab, or the review prompt. About 5 minutes. Adds to the week; never asks for a full rebuild.

Do and tick, every day

Open Home, tick what you did

Home, Today: the next block, one tick each, the ring. About 2 minutes including the optional journal line.

Review, once a week

Read the week, close it, plan the next

Recap in Progress: one closing line, "Done for the week", then "Plan next week". About 5 minutes.

Time budget: 2 minutes a day and 10 a week

Design budget, not a measurement. The daily minutes are the tick; the weekly minutes are planning and review. Bar drawn to scale over one week, 10 minutes of planning and review plus 14 minutes of daily ticks.

Daily ticks, 2 min a day (14 a week) Plan and review, 10 min a week

Open question for the owner: if "10 a week" meant 10 minutes in total, the daily budget falls to about 1 minute. Until decided, the map uses 2 minutes a day and 10 minutes of planning and review on top.
